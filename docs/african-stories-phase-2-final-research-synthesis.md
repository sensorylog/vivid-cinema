# African Stories — Phase 2 final research synthesis and exit decision

**Research cut-off:** 25 November 2026  
**Decision:** Phase 2 desk-research exit criteria met; proceed to Phase 3 blueprint.  
**Boundary:** This closes research for product planning. It does not certify title-level rights, partner agreements, catalogue ingestion or playback authorization.

## 1. Executive decision

The evidence supports a continental, rights-aware discovery and context platform spanning African cinema, documentaries, oral histories, newsreels, archival footage and historical screen culture. African Stories should combine canonical work records, linked people and organizations, archive holdings, official external viewing routes, territory-specific licensed playback, editorial context and provenance.

The evidence does not support promising a particular number of playable films, a universal archive API, or a single pan-African streaming licence. The platform must remain useful when a work has no playable route.

- [x] Distinguish archive custody, research access, digitization, reproduction permission, public viewing and commercial VOD rights.
- [x] Identify national/regional archive, cinematheque and diaspora-custodian leads across all five African regions.

## 4. Market and competitor findings

The ecosystem is fragmented across streaming platforms with selected licensed catalogues; broadcaster VOD and catch-up services; commercial sales catalogues for approved buyers; festival databases without rights; national archives with physical holdings and controlled research access; and specialist Pan-African/diaspora archives.

This supports African Stories as a cross-source discovery, provenance and context layer. It does not support copying competitor catalogues or building an unlicensed universal player.

Selected official operator evidence:
- **AfriDocs:** describes a free growing documentary catalogue for Africa and states that streamed films are geo-blocked for Africa. Treat as official destination/partner lead, not a Vivid source.
- **Signature TV:** official FAQ describes transactional rentals, including 72-hour episode rentals, seven-day feature rentals and 14-day season rentals. Useful model for access expiry; no Vivid resale/embed rights found.
- **kweliTV:** partner page markets a global Black-cinema catalogue and subscription/rental/free-channel access. Third-party catalogue integration and title-specific rights remain unverified.
- **SABC+:** public broadcaster OTT is a model for catch-up and broadcaster-owned catalogues; access and rights are not assumed to extend outside South Africa or to third-party players.
- **MultiChoice Studios:** official pages describe B2B content sales/distribution, a large African content library, catalogues and secure preview access for approved buyers/distributors. Licensing cost depends on rights required. It is a commercial acquisition lead, not an open playback API.
- **Ergo Distribution International:** live catalogue displays title-level country availability and offers screeners/bookings by territory. Strong pattern for territory-aware rights discovery and a potential sales-agent contact.
- **Okada Media:** official site describes pan-African film sales/distribution and a curated licensable catalogue. Verify exact title mandate and rights window during any approach.
- **Cinémathèque Afrique / IFcinéma:** official catalogue exposes 1,751 records, with 630 titles shown as having ongoing screening rights for its stated non-commercial screening context. This does not establish Vivid commercial streaming rights.
- **True Continent:** official pages describe a documentary aggregator/sales model, while its homepage labels its streaming platform “Coming Soon”. Treat as prospective lead, not a functioning playback source until independently verified.
- **Africa in Motion African Film Database:** archived pages described 1,500+ records and explicitly state the festival/database does not own the films or grant screening rights. Useful for discovery, not playback.

## 5. Rights and legal conclusions for product design

This is an engineering/product control summary, not legal advice. Each launch territory requires qualified local counsel and title-specific review.

1. Rights are bundles, not a yes/no field. A film can contain separate script, music, performance, footage, photograph, archive, translation, subtitle, poster and distribution rights.
2. Access is not exploitation. Reading-room appointments, research copies, digitized preservation masters and archive catalogue records do not imply reproduction, public communication, embedding, download, monetization or VOD rights.
3. Territory and term matter. A licence must specify allowed/excluded territories, start/end dates, windows, holdbacks, platform, devices, monetization and language versions.
4. Archive custody and copyright ownership can differ. The archive may preserve a physical carrier but not own the film copyright or all embedded rights.
5. Public-domain analysis must be work- and jurisdiction-specific. Do not assume old, colonial-era, government-produced, anonymous or archived footage is automatically public domain. Moral rights, performers, folklore, privacy, personality, cultural heritage and contract restrictions may remain relevant.
6. Official external playback is a distinct state. A lawful official watch route can be linked when permitted, but it does not confer Vivid embedding or commercial rights.
7. Embargo and preservation constraints must be machine-readable. NARSSA describes embargoed material, access copies instead of originals, copyright restrictions and fees/technical feasibility for copies.

### Priority legal-review matrix

| Market | Primary law / authority lead | Counsel questions |
|---|---|---|

| Nigeria | Copyright Act 2022; Nigerian Copyright Commission | Audiovisual ownership and communication/making available, collective licensing, music/performer rights, term and cross-border VOD. |
| Kenya | Copyright Act 2001 as amended; Kenya Copyright Board | Audiovisual exclusive rights include communication to public and broadcasting; assess making available, licensing, moral rights, performers and archive reproductions. |
| South Africa | Copyright Act 1978 as amended; DSAC / NFVSA and relevant IP authority | Film reproduction, public showing, broadcast, archive access, reproduction charges, performer/music/underlying work rights and current legislative amendments. |
| Cameroon | Law No. 2000/010 on archives; applicable copyright legislation and MINAC | Archive access restrictions, legal deposit, recipient restrictions on reproduction/dissemination/commercial use, copyright and neighbouring rights. |
| Mozambique | INICC archive/film procedures; applicable copyright and cultural-sector regulations | Written archive requests, purpose and territory-specific fees, reproduction, screening, VOD and licensing authority. |
| Tunisia, Morocco, Egypt, Algeria, Burkina Faso and other launch markets | National copyright statutes and relevant cinematheque/archive rules | Local-language rights opinions, public communication/VOD, moral and performer rights, archive/reproduction terms, classification and privacy rules. |

### Minimum rights evidence before playback

- licensor identity and authority, chain-of-title documents and exact work/version;
- explicit rights granted: host, stream, embed, communicate to public, promote, distribute, sublicense, download/offline, clip/trailer;
- permitted territories, term, window, holdbacks, platform/domain, devices and monetization;
- included/excluded music, footage, stills, posters, subtitles, translations and performer rights;
- DRM, geo-blocking, concurrency, watermarking, security and technical delivery;
- fees, minimum guarantees, revenue share, reporting, audit, taxes and payment;
- warranties, indemnity, attribution, moral-rights approvals, takedown, termination and expiry;
- contract reference, reviewer, approval date, renewal date and evidence attachments.

## 7. Architecture decisions carried into Phase 3

### Canonical domain model

Phase 3 must design separate entities for:
1. **Work:** canonical intellectual work/title.
2. **Edition/version:** cut, restoration, language version, runtime and format.
3. **Person/organization:** filmmaker, producer, cast, archive, distributor, rights holder and institution.
4. **Place/period/subject:** production country, story geography, archive custody location, historical period and themes.
5. **Source record:** metadata source, archive catalogue, festival listing, external database and provenance.
6. **Holding:** institution custody, carrier/format, identifier, digitization status, access restrictions and physical condition where known.
7. **Asset:** poster, still, trailer, subtitle or artwork with independent rights and attribution.
8. **Access route:** official external destination, reading-room request, public screening, partner preview or Vivid playback route.
9. **Rights grant:** licensor, scope, territory, term, platform, monetization, restrictions, evidence and review state.
10. **Playback provider:** provider identity, approved integration type, provider title ID, technical health and contract reference.
11. **Editorial record:** fact-check sources, context, uncertainty, sensitivity notes, corrections and reviewer.

### Access-state machine

Use distinct states, not a boolean:
- research_only
- archive_record
- access_pending
- watch_officially
- licensed_embed
- stream_on_vivid
- expired_or_suspended

Transitions require evidence and approval. Licensed embed requires a written licence expressly authorizing embedding on named Vivid domains; Vivid streaming requires the relevant hosting and public-communication/VOD rights. A technical URL alone never advances a title’s rights state.

### Third-player boundary

- Independent African player runtime and provider registry; no reuse of movie/anime provider assumptions.
- Provider adapters are allowlisted and contract-linked.
- Resolve identity using stable work/version IDs, not title string alone.
- Check territory, dates, window, monetization and access mode before creating a playback session.
- Keep credentials/secrets server-side; validate embed domains and signed delivery requirements.
- Do not extract hidden stream URLs or bypass DRM, geo-blocks, authentication, paywalls or domain restrictions.
- Support only authorized iframe, partner SDK, signed HLS/DASH or other documented delivery.
- Health-check sources and fail closed to official link, archive record or access-pending UI.
- Log entitlement decision, provider, region, version and failure category without exposing secrets.
- Test captions/subtitles, audio-language selection, accessibility, resume and device compatibility separately.

### Editorial and provenance boundary

Title pages must distinguish historical fact supported by sources; filmmaker/archivist/participant testimony; documentary interpretation; dramatized reconstruction; and fictional historical drama. Preserve source citations, date confidence, alternate spellings, transliterations and corrections. Provide content notes for traumatic or sensitive material and a process for contested historical claims. AI-generated metadata is never authoritative without human review.

## 8. Phase 3 input decisions

Recommended defaults for the blueprint:
1. Primary product is discovery + contextual knowledge + rights-aware official viewing, not a promise of universal streaming.
2. A work may be catalogued with a verified source record even if no stream is available; its access state is explicit.
3. Only licensed embeds and Vivid-controlled streams enter the African player.
4. Geography is continental and diaspora-oriented; production country, subject geography, archive custodian and rights territory remain separate.
5. Content types include fiction, documentary, short, feature, series, oral history, newsreel, archival footage, educational/public-information film, animation and experimental work.
6. Original audio, dubbed audio, subtitles, title aliases and transliteration are independent fields.
7. Design for licensed AVOD, SVOD, TVOD, institutional/educational and free official destinations, but activate no model without matching rights.
8. Begin with a curated, verifiable cohort across multiple regions; set no catalogue-size target until rights and access data justify it.
