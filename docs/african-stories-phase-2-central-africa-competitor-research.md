# African Stories — Phase 2 research supplement: Central Africa, archive access and competitor landscape

**Research cut-off:** 25 November 2026  
**Status:** Evidence supplement; not a rights clearance, partner commitment, or final product specification.  
**Programme:** Phases 2–7 remain on PR #524, `feat/african-stories-phases-2-7`.

## 1. Executive findings

1. **Central Africa is not an empty-content region.** Cameroon has a government-defined national cinematheque/audiovisual archive mandate and a statutory legal-deposit framework. The Democratic Republic of the Congo has a discoverable national archives institution (INACO), although its online portal and operational status need independent verification. Historical Congolese works also appear in the Cinémathèque Afrique / Institut français catalogue and specialist documentary databases.
2. **Archive discovery and commercial playback remain separate.** Cameroon’s archive law explicitly preserves copyright and neighbouring rights and says access to archive documents does not itself authorize reproduction, dissemination or commercial use. This is a direct reason to keep catalogue records, official external links, research access and Vivid playback as separate states.
3. **The Cinémathèque Afrique is a major cross-border acquisition lead.** Institut français describes a catalogue of more than 1,700 titles, including over 600 royalty-free titles for non-commercial distribution. “Royalty-free” must not be interpreted as commercial, worldwide, perpetual, platform, or embed rights. Each title and intended use still needs written confirmation.
4. **The competitor set is broader than one pan-African streaming app.** It includes documentary services (AfriDocs), broadcaster VOD (SABC+), transactional African film services (Signature TV), global Black-cinema services (kweliTV, AfroLandTV), local/national services, and archive/library catalogues. Their business models, geographies and rights positions differ.
5. **Market intelligence must be dated and verified.** A November 2026 industry tracker reports significant platform and distribution changes, including a Showmax closure announcement and market-specific launches. Treat it as a lead generator, not a primary legal or operational source; verify any competitive assumption against the platform/operator before product decisions.

### Cameroon: statutory evidence that changes the product model

MINAC’s official pages identify the national cinematheque and audiovisual archive responsibilities. Its legal-deposit material defines audiovisual documents as moving-image records, with or without sound, and requires specified broadcast materials to be deposited with the National Cinematheque.

More importantly, Cameroon Law No. 2000/010 of 19 December 2000 on archives provides that:
- public archives are part of national cultural heritage;
- access to archives is generally free subject to statutory exceptions and access periods;
- consultation may occur on site or remotely and copies may be issued;
- communication of archive documents remains subject to copyright and neighbouring rights; and
- communication excludes reproduction, dissemination or commercial use by the recipient and third parties.

**Product consequence:** even where an archive record is consultable, African Stories must not infer permission to embed, redistribute, monetize, download or mirror the work. A separate written grant is required for the specific exploitation mode.

## 3. Cross-border catalogue and title-discovery opportunities

### Cinémathèque Afrique — Institut français

Institut français describes a collection of more than 1,700 African film titles from the 1960s onward, with over 600 titles described as royalty-free for non-commercial distribution. The public-facing LaCinetek presentation exposes individual film records, including works from Republic of the Congo, Niger, Senegal, Burkina Faso and Tunisia.

**Why this matters:** this is a high-value catalogue and rights-holder discovery partner, not simply a streaming API. A first partnership conversation should ask for:
- machine-readable catalogue export or API terms;
- stable identifiers, title variants, original-language titles and transliterations;
- director, cast, country, year, runtime, synopsis, format and restoration/version metadata;
- which titles are actually digitized and available for viewing;
- exact definition of “royalty-free” and the permitted non-commercial uses;
- whether commercial VOD, ad-supported VOD, subscription VOD, embeds, clips, educational use or territorial sublicensing can be negotiated;
- rights holder / licensor identity and chain-of-title evidence per title;
- territory, term, platform, language/subtitle, DRM and reporting requirements.

Do not bulk-ingest images, posters, synopses or film files without confirming reuse rights and technical terms.

### Additional discovery sources

- **Africa in Motion African Film Database:** historically described a searchable database of 1,500+ African films by title, country, language, director, year, genre and theme. Its own disclaimer says the database does not hold rights and cannot grant screening permission. Use for discovery only; recheck current availability and terms.
- **Africa Documentary Film Fund / Documentary Africa (ADEPt):** searchable records include historical documentary and feature titles from the DRC and other countries. Treat as metadata leads; verify whether the record owner has authority to grant rights.
- **Yale African Film Guide:** a useful curated discovery index for archives, databases, streaming services and research resources. It is a secondary research guide, not a rights source.
- **Library of Congress public moving-image archive directory:** useful for finding institutional repositories, including African and diaspora holdings. Directory inclusion does not establish a digitized catalogue or open rights.

### Competitor implication

African Stories should not attempt to out-volume established subscription/rental services in their own licensed catalogues. Its defensible position is the **discovery and context layer across African cinema and moving-image heritage**:
- one searchable, carefully sourced record for a work;
- provenance, people, place, language, historical period and subject indexing;
- a clear status distinguishing archive record, official external viewing, licensed partner playback and Vivid-licensed streaming;
- a rights-respecting route to the best currently verified way to watch;
- curated paths across films, documentaries, newsreels, oral histories, archival footage and historical context.

This can be valuable even when the title is not playable inside Vivid. The interface must never disguise a catalogue record as an available stream.

## 6. Technical and data implications

The evidence supports the existing proposed state machine:

- `research_only`: a research lead or repository record; no public viewing claim.
- `archive_record`: a verified work or holding is catalogued; playback not established.
- `access_pending`: a request or rights inquiry is in progress.
- `watch_officially`: an authorized public destination has been checked; Vivid does not host or claim the stream.
- `licensed_embed`: written rights expressly permit the named third-party player/embed on Vivid under defined terms.
- `stream_on_vivid`: written rights expressly permit Vivid-hosted or controlled VOD delivery for the relevant territory, term and monetization.
- `expired_or_suspended`: playback disabled when rights expire, are withdrawn, or a compliance issue is raised.

Required separate entities:
- canonical work and alternate title records;
- source/repository records;
- archive holding and physical/digital format;
- viewing-access route;
- rights grant and restrictions;
- playback provider and technical endpoint;
- territory-specific availability;
- provenance and editorial citations.

**Never collapse these into a single `is_streamable` boolean.** A title can be catalogued, digitized, viewable in a reading room, externally available, or licensed for one territory while remaining unavailable for Vivid playback elsewhere.

## 7. Phase 2 gaps that remain open

Phase 2 is **not complete**. The following are required before Phase 3 architecture is considered evidence-ready:

1. Verify the Cameroon MINAC contact and current state of the National Cinematheque, catalogue access and digitization programme.
2. Verify INACO’s legal identity, portal operator, catalogue coverage, and whether it has moving-image collections.
3. Obtain a written response from Cinémathèque Afrique on catalogue access, metadata reuse, non-commercial title list and commercial licensing pathways.
4. Expand the country-by-country register across the rest of Central, West, East, North and Southern Africa; include diaspora custodians and European repositories holding African works.
5. Test a representative set of titles for official public viewing routes, with dates and territory, and record failures/geo-blocks rather than assuming availability.
6. Verify competitor operational status, target countries, catalogue model, rental/subscription/ad-supported model, API/partner programme and third-party integration terms directly with operators.
7. Build a title-level rights evidence sample across documentary, fiction, newsreel, oral-history and archive footage categories.
8. Obtain local counsel review questions for Ghana and priority target markets; this research document is not legal advice.
9. Define an editorial policy for archival footage with colonial, racist, violent, traumatic or otherwise sensitive historical material, including provenance, context, content notes and correction procedures.
