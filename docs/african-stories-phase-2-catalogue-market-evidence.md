## 2. Critical reconciliation: FESPACO collection figures

Two official African Cinematheque/FESPACO pages currently present different figures:
- The FESPACO African Film Library presentation says more than 1,000 works are treated in storage, including documentary, fiction, news, short and feature works representative of African regions: https://fespaco.bf/the-african-film-library/
- The African Cinematheque conservation page says its collection includes more than 12,000 films across national and international works and multiple analogue/digital formats, with digitisation/restoration ongoing: https://cinemathequeafricaine.org/en/conservation-room/

These figures may represent different reporting dates, collection scopes, units or stages of processing. Until the institution reconciles them in writing, Vivid must not publish either as a single verified count of accessible African films, much less playable films.

**Data rule:** distinguish total holdings, catalogued records, physically inspectable items, digitised masters, access copies, publicly viewable items, licensed items and playable items. These are separate metrics.

## 3. Festival data: useful but not a streaming feed

AFRIFF's 2026 programme and Encounters' programme pages show that festivals publish structured information that can support discovery: title, director, synopsis, category, premiere status, country/region and event edition. These records are valuable for:
- identifying contemporary and historical African works;
- linking directors, producers, cinematographers and subjects;
- tracking premiere, award and festival histories;
- identifying rights holders and sales agents;
- mapping thematic collections and regional/language gaps.

They should be stored as event/screening records attached to a canonical work, not treated as the work's streaming availability. Festival screening agreements are typically event-specific; the programme page alone does not reveal whether Vivid can show the film after the festival.

## 4. Distributor and platform market signals

The market is fragmented by business model. The following should be evaluated separately:

### Curated / broadcaster-backed documentary access
- **AfriDocs / Steps / Bertha Foundation:** official page describes a free growing documentary streaming catalogue for Africa and says streamed films are geo-blocked for Africa. https://www.afridocs.net/about/ This demonstrates territory-specific availability and a strong editorial/distribution partner lead. It does not give Vivid embed or sublicensing rights.
- **Encounters:** festival and industry ecosystem with film programmes and filmmaker/industry activity. It is a discovery and relationship channel, not assumed to be a distributor.

### Commercial catalogue sales
- **MultiChoice Studios:** official FAQ describes a large sales/distribution catalogue and says approved registered broadcasters/distributors can access limited viewing behind secure login; licensing cost depends on the rights required. https://www.multichoicestudios.com/faq This is a B2B licensing path, not an open public player feed.

### Filmmaker-first and emerging distribution
- **TuWatch Africa:** its site describes filmmaker submissions, non-exclusive rights retention and a revenue share. https://tuwatch.africa/about These are platform self-descriptions; diligence actual legal entity, contract, catalogue, payment history, territories and technical integration before any partnership.
- **Kora Stream Africa:** describes a catalogue of African films, series, documentaries and creator/partner channels, with rights-holder submission review. https://streamkoraafrica.com/about Treat as an emerging prospect; verify service operation, catalogue and rights contracts.
- **True Continent:** its filmmaker page describes a non-exclusive African documentary aggregation and sales model; its homepage labels streaming as coming soon. https://truecontinent.com/filmmakers and https://truecontinent.com/ Do not treat the advertised future catalogue or revenue share as an existing playable source.
- **African Alliance Films:** invites submissions for distribution and says selected films proceed to a tailored distribution agreement. https://www.african-films.com/submit-film This is a lead for negotiation, not an open content catalogue.

### Research and metadata databases
- **Africine / FEPACI critics ecosystem:** surfaced in university research guides as a film criticism and discovery database. Before using it as a data source, verify the current canonical service, catalogue scope, API/export, ownership and reuse licence.
- **Africa in Motion's African Film Database:** historical archive page explicitly disclaims rights ownership. This is an important warning against treating any aggregated film database as a licensing authority.

## 5. Practical content-sourcing funnel

For each candidate work:

1. **Discover:** identify title from archive, festival, broadcaster, distributor, filmmaker or research source.
2. **Resolve identity:** confirm original title, alternate spellings, year, country/region, director, work type and version.
3. **Establish provenance:** record where the metadata and assets came from, when checked and source confidence.
4. **Find authority:** identify producer, current rightsholder, sales agent, archive custodian and any other relevant rights owner.
5. **Determine access:** archive research access, official public watch route, licensed embed, Vivid-hosted licence or no known route.
6. **Clear rights:** obtain written grant for the exact use, territory, period, platform, monetization and assets.
7. **Validate delivery:** test the approved provider route, captions, language, playback, geography and expiry.
8. **Publish correct state:** catalogue-only, archive record, official watch, licensed embed or Vivid stream.

Do not collapse these stages into one “source found” flag.

## 6. Research conclusions that should constrain Phase 3

- The product's core entity is a **work**, not a provider URL. One work can have many source records, archive holdings, screenings, editions, rights grants and access routes.
- A **screening/event** must be a separate entity from the film; festival history is valuable and time-bound.
- **Rights grants** must be versioned, territorial and temporal, and should not be embedded as simple booleans on a film record.
- **Archive holding** must be distinct from **digital access copy** and **commercial distribution permission**.
- The interface should make “Discover”, “Archive record”, “Watch officially”, “Access pending” and “Stream on Vivid” useful outcomes, rather than making all non-streaming records look broken.
- Search should support multilingual title aliases, transliteration, people, country/region, language, era, format, topic and source institution.
- A partner source should not become a provider until the exact rights and technical integration have passed onboarding.
