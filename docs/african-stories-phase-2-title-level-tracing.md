# African Stories — Phase 2 Title-Level Source Tracing

**Research date:** 25 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Demonstrate the difference between festival discovery, rightsholder discovery, official viewing and Vivid playback authorization. These are research examples, not a launch-ready title catalogue.

## Case 1 — The Eyes of Ghana (2026)

**Identity and context**
- Official film website describes the feature documentary directed by Ben Proudfoot, following Ghanaian cinematographer Chris Hesse and his preservation of footage documenting Kwame Nkrumah, Ghana's independence and African liberation movements.
- The official site describes a trove of more than 1,300 films and has a prominent “Watch Now Free on YouTube” link.
- Official film site: https://theeyesofghana.com/
- The official site is stronger evidence for the official viewing route than third-party streaming aggregators. A Ghana Broadcasting Corporation report dated 18 November 2026 also reports the free YouTube release and identifies the producer/production partnership: https://www.gbcghanaonline.com/entertainment/the-eyes-of-ghana/2026/3/

**Access determination**
- Official external watch route: indicated by the film's own website as free on YouTube.
- Vivid embed/rehosting rights: not established by the public watch link.
- Vivid should link to the official film site/watch route unless a separate written embed/distribution licence is obtained.
- The official website currently contains press asset links; poster, still and trailer rights must be evaluated separately before reusing those assets.

**Research significance**
- This is a Ghana-centred example where a major historical audiovisual archive and a new documentary create a high-value editorial path: film → Chris Hesse → Kwame Nkrumah → Ghana independence → African liberation movements → archival reels.
- Do not infer that Vivid can separately stream the underlying 1,300 archival films because they appear in or are discussed by this documentary. Each archival work has its own custody, provenance and rights chain.

## Case 2 — Amílcar (2025)

**Identity and context**
- Production company Mosaic describes Amílcar as an 89-minute creative documentary about Amílcar Cabral, filmed in 16mm and using unpublished archival material and an imagined diary based on Cabral's writings.
- The production page identifies original languages as Portuguese, French, Creole and Spanish, and names co-producers and financing bodies: https://mosaicprod.com/en/projects/amilcar/
- The production page identifies ODD Slice Films as sales agent and provides a sales contact route.
- Sales-agent page: https://www.oddslicefilms.com/amilcar

**Access determination**
- Rights-holder/sales-agent lead: identified.
- Public online viewing route: not established by the production page reviewed.
- Vivid licensing route: enquire with the named sales agent and verify rights by territory, term, language and platform.
- This is a strong example of why a film record needs original languages, co-production details, archival-source provenance and sales-agent relations—not only a title and poster.

## Case 3 — The Woman Who Poked the Leopard

**Identity and context**
- Official film website describes a documentary about Ugandan academic and activist Stella Nyanzi, directed by Patience Nitumwesiga, and provides a direct contact email and Kampala location: https://www.thewomanwhopokedtheleopard.com/
- The film's festival presence can be discovered through programme records, but the film's own website is the better route for filmmaker/rightsholder contact.

**Access determination**
- Rightsholder/production contact lead: identified on official site.
- Public streaming route: not established by the official site reviewed.
- Vivid licensing route: contact production/rightsholder; request territory-specific rights and current sales/distribution representation.
- Editorial treatment: sensitive political history and a living/modern subject require careful fact-checking, context and defamation/privacy review. Do not present promotional synopsis as independent historical verification.

## Case 4 — Festival programme record versus rights

- AFRIFF's 2026 programme lists 119 films across nine categories: https://afriff.com/program
- Encounters publishes film programmes, synopses and premiere labels: https://encounters.co.za/films/
- These records are useful for discovery and event history. They do not establish a post-festival VOD licence, embed permission, rightsholder identity or availability in Ghana/other territories.

## Conclusions from sample tracing

| Work | Identity source | Rightsholder/contact lead | Official public watch route found? | Vivid playback rights established? | Appropriate state today |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Eyes of Ghana | Official film site | Production/film site; chain-of-title still to be confirmed for Vivid use | Yes — official site advertises free YouTube viewing | No | watch_officially |
| Amílcar | Production company | Named sales agent | Not established from reviewed primary page | No | access_pending |
| The Woman Who Poked the Leopard | Official film site | Production contact | Not established from reviewed primary page | No | access_pending |

State assignment is limited to the evidence reviewed on the research date and must be rechecked before launch. “watch_officially” means link out to the official destination; it does not mean embed or copy the video.

## Follow-up research actions

1. For The Eyes of Ghana, confirm the canonical YouTube destination, whether the film's official page embeds it or links out, geographic availability, captions/subtitles, and whether official embedding is enabled. Request written permission before any Vivid-hosted embed or asset reuse.
2. For Amílcar, contact the listed sales agent for rights availability, sales territories, term, VOD/platform rights, educational rights, language versions, trailer/still assets and screening history.
3. For The Woman Who Poked the Leopard, confirm current sales representation and available territories/platforms, request a screener through the official production contact, and record its festival history with dates.
4. Build a repeatable title sample across all five African regions and diaspora, including fiction, documentary, animation, oral history, archival newsreel and educational works.
5. Keep a separate rights review for every film and every archive clip used within a film. A documentary's licence does not automatically transfer rights to its underlying archival materials.

**Phase 2 remains open.** Three traced examples are not representative enough to close the continent-wide source and access research.
