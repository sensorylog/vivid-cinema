# African Stories — Phase 2 Research Register

**Status:** Phase 2 in progress  
**Research snapshot:** 25 November 2026  
**Scope:** Pan-African cinema, documentary, audiovisual heritage, historical storytelling, authorized discovery and playback.

This is an evidence register, not a claim that every listed collection is streamable or that Vivid has secured any rights. Public catalogue records, archive holdings, public-domain status, online availability, embed permission and commercial streaming rights are separate facts and must be verified title by title.

## 1. Early findings that shape the product

1. **Build a discovery and access graph, not just a streaming catalogue.** African film heritage is distributed across national archives, specialist film libraries, universities, broadcasters, festivals, rights holders and commercial distributors. Some holdings are viewable only onsite or by research request; some have no digitised copy.
2. **Represent access honestly.** Each title needs an explicit access state: `stream_on_vivid` (Vivid has documented rights and a working authorized playback route), `watch_officially` (a verified official external viewing route), `archive_record` (catalogue/holding record but no public viewing route verified), or `access_pending` (rights or access being investigated). Do not infer playability from a poster, metadata record, archive holding or public URL.
3. **Treat territorial rights as first-class data.** AfriDocs states its streamed films are geo-blocked for Africa; this is a concrete example that a viewing route can be territory-limited. Record allowed/blocked territories, term, language, platform, monetization, exclusivity and sublicensing separately.
4. **A third player should be a rights-aware resolver, not a universal scraper.** It should accept only approved provider records and authorized embeds/streams, check territory and license windows, verify provider health, and fail closed to a clear official-link/archive state when rights or playback cannot be confirmed.
5. **The history layer must distinguish evidence types.** Documentary, oral history, archival newsreel, historical footage, dramatization and fictional historical drama must be labeled distinctly, with source/provenance, date confidence and editorial context.

## 3. Metadata and playback research

### Metadata is not playback permission

- TMDB's official FAQ distinguishes non-commercial developer use from commercial use: commercial projects should contact TMDB for licensing; attribution is required for permitted API use. Vivid must review its own commercial status and existing terms before reusing TMDB assets or data in African Stories: https://developer.themoviedb.org/docs/faq
- Wikidata's SPARQL service provides structured, machine-readable entity discovery. It is useful for identity resolution and cross-linking people, countries, works and dates, but is not a substitute for authoritative film records or image/video rights: https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:SPARQL_query_service/API
- The FIAF and Library of Congress directories can seed institution discovery, but their records must be reconciled with each archive's current catalogue and access policy.

### Authorized playback examples and constraints

- YouTube provides an official IFrame Player API for embedding and controlling videos. A YouTube embed is only appropriate when the specific video is lawfully published and the uploader/rightsholder has made it available under applicable platform settings and rights: https://developers.google.com/youtube/iframe_api_reference
- Vimeo documents embeddability and domain-level privacy controls. A title may be embeddable only on whitelisted domains; the provider's embed/privacy settings must be respected: https://help.vimeo.com/hc/en-us/articles/12426259908881-How-to-embed-my-video
- Vimeo OTT distinguishes trailer embeds from subscription playback and supports API authorizations for authorized embeds. This is a possible rights-holder-hosted integration pattern, not a universal source of third-party catalogue playback: https://help.vimeo.com/hc/en-us/articles/12427027508881-How-embedding-works-on-Vimeo-OTT
- Provider integrations must never bypass DRM, geo-blocks, authentication, domain restrictions, paywalls or access controls. A public link or working iframe alone is not proof that Vivid has permission to rehost, monetize or redistribute the work.

## 4. Competitor and ecosystem observations

The landscape is not a single direct competitor set. It includes:
- **Curated documentary access:** AfriDocs.
- **Archive and heritage custodians:** FESPACO African Film Library, national archives, university collections and Pan-African archives.
- **Commercial catalogue sales:** MultiChoice Studios and specialist distributors.
- **Filmmaker-first distribution:** Laba Cinema and TuWatch Africa (both require current operational and contract diligence).
- **Emerging African streaming brands:** Kora Stream Africa and other services whose catalogue and rights must be verified.

Vivid's defensible product position should therefore be broader than “another African Netflix”: a trustworthy African screen-heritage discovery layer combining film, documentary, oral history, archival records, people, places, timelines, contextual essays and official viewing routes—with direct streaming only where Vivid has documented authorization.

## 5. Required title-level evidence model

Before a title can enter an active playable state, store and review at least:

- Canonical title ID; original/local/international titles; aliases; work type; country/region; languages; release/production date and confidence.
- People/organizations with roles; synopsis and editorial description; historical period/place; tags; related works and collections.
- Metadata source, source URL, retrieval date, source record ID, attribution requirements and confidence.
- Asset provenance for poster/stills/trailer; asset licence/permission and expiry.
- Rights holder and chain-of-title evidence; contract/document reference; rights granted (host, stream, embed, promote, distribute, sublicense); territory allow/deny list; term start/end; language; platform; monetization; exclusivity; DRM; geo-block and domain constraints.
- Playback provider, provider title ID, approved embed/stream type, allowed domains, authentication requirements, source health, last verification, fallback route and takedown state.
- Editorial evidence sources, fact-check state, uncertainty notes, corrections history and distinction between factual record and dramatization.

No title should be promoted to `stream_on_vivid` without a documented rights basis, territory/window check and a tested authorized playback route.

## 7. Evidence quality rules

- Prefer primary institution, rights-holder, distributor and official API documentation.
- Record publication/update date and access date. Recheck volatile pages before launch.
- Separate verified facts from claims made by a service about itself.
- A catalogue count is not a playable-title count.
- A publicly viewable title is not automatically licensed for Vivid's commercial use.
- No scraping of protected catalogues or extracting hidden playback URLs.
- When rights/access are unknown, display “Access pending” or “Archive record”; do not invent availability.
