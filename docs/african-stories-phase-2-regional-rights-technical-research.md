# African Stories — Phase 2 Regional, Rights and Technical Research

**Research date:** 25 November 2026  
**Programme PR:** https://github.com/sensorylog/vivid-cinema/pull/524  
**Status:** Evidence expansion; Phase 2 remains open

## Executive findings

1. **The archive landscape is real but fragmented and access-controlled.** Strong primary evidence exists for national audiovisual collections in Nigeria, South Africa and Mozambique; Ghana has an identified archive initiative and a National Film Authority remit; Uganda has a rediscovered Film Unit collection under a digitisation and restitution project; Eritrea's Research and Documentation Centre is digitising audiovisual and historical holdings.
2. **Access and rights are separate.** Mozambique's INICC requires written requests and conditional authorization for archive-image use, with differentiated fees by use and geography. South Africa's NARSSA makes access subject to preservation, copyright and other restrictions. Archive access does not establish copyright ownership.
3. **Rights clearance is a launch requirement.** WIPO's 2026 guide explains chain-of-title, multiple embedded rights and territorial/temporal scope. Ghana's Copyright Act protects communication to the public and folklore; Nigeria's Copyright Act 2022 expressly reserves communication and making available of audiovisual works to the public.
4. **The existing Vivid movie/anime provider list is not an African Stories provider catalogue.** The repository's own audit says its third-party embed audit does not establish real-time availability or rights. The direct-stream engine is deliberately disabled without an authorized resolver and allowlisted hosts. African Stories needs a separate, rights-aware provider registry.
5. **The opportunity is larger than streaming.** Archive records, research access, licensed clips, official external links, festival records, oral histories, educational collections and fully licensed playback all need first-class states.

## 2. Rights and legal findings

### WIPO baseline — chain of title and territory

WIPO's 2026 Rights Clearance guide explains that audiovisual works may contain multiple separately protected materials and that clearance establishes chain of title and reduces the risk of withdrawal or infringement. It explains that copyright is territorial and the relevant law can depend on where a work is exploited. https://www.wipo.int/web-publications/rights-clearance-a-guide-for-independent-filmmakers/en/2-rights-clearance-from-idea-to-distribution.html

A rights packet must consider:
- underlying screenplay/story/adaptation;
- film producer and audiovisual copyright;
- director/author and moral rights;
- performers and performance fixations;
- music composition, lyrics, sound recording and synchronization/master use;
- archive footage, photographs, artwork, maps and third-party clips;
- broadcaster/newsreel rights;
- subtitles, dubbing, translations and accessibility tracks;
- posters, stills and promotional assets;
- privacy, personality/image, location and cultural permissions.

### Ghana — Copyright Act 2005 (Act 690)

Ghana's Act provides exclusive economic rights including reproduction, adaptation, public performance, broadcasting and communication to the public. It separately protects folklore, including communication to the public, and includes performer rights. Sources:
- https://ghalii.org/akn/gh/act/2005/690/eng%402009-12-31
- https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/1789

Product implication: traditional cultural expressions are not “free material”. Community/folklore rights, custodianship, consent, attribution and culturally appropriate use need a dedicated review path. Do not assume an old recording is public domain because it is old or held by an institution.

### Nigeria — Copyright Act 2022 (Act No. 8)

The Nigerian Act 2022 commenced 17 March 2023 and lists audiovisual works as copyright-eligible. Section 11 reserves rights including communication to the public and making works available by wire or wireless means at a time and place chosen by the user. Sources:
- Nigerian Copyright Commission PDF: https://copyright.gov.ng/CopyrightAct/CopyrightAct2023FinalPublication1.pdf
- WIPO Lex: https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/21820

Product implication: discoverability on a public site does not authorize Vivid's own on-demand making-available service. Secure a licence from the party holding the relevant rights.

### Archive access is independent of copyright ownership

South Africa's NARSSA says access is subject to archive conditions, preservation, copyright and other restrictions; original film may not be available for direct viewing; reproduction depends on condition, feasibility, rights and charges. https://www.nationalarchives.gov.za/faq-2

Mozambique's INICC requires written requests and authorization for archive-image use, with differentiated conditions and rates by intended use and territory. https://inicc.gov.mz/en/arquivodefilmes

Product implication: physical custody, research viewing, reproduction permission and exploitation rights are separate records. An archive may own a physical carrier without owning copyright; a rightsholder may own copyright without holding the preservation master.

### Rights-state machine — recommended

| State | Meaning | Vivid action |
|---|---|---|
| research_only | Candidate title/source identified; no confirmed access or rights | Internal research only |
| archive_record | Source confirms a catalogue/holding record; public playback not verified | Metadata with archive citation and access note |
| access_pending | Research/viewing/permission request submitted or chain under investigation | Metadata and explicit pending status; no playback |
| watch_officially | Current official external viewing route verified for viewer territory | Link to official destination; no rehosting |
| licensed_embed | Written permission covers embedding on Vivid domain for specified territory/term/use and embed works | Approved embed within scope |
| stream_on_vivid | Written rights cover Vivid-hosted/on-demand distribution, territory, term, monetization and delivery; playback QA passed | Vivid player with server-side entitlement/territory/window checks |
| expired_or_suspended | Licence expired, revoked, takedown or source suspended | Disable playback; preserve audit record |

Rights state must derive from a reviewed evidence packet, not a free-form catalogue-editor toggle.

### Minimum rights record

Record licensor identity and authority; chain-of-title evidence; work/version/episode; included/excluded elements; rights granted (metadata, artwork, trailer, clip, embed, public performance, VOD, download, hosting, promotion, sublicensing); territory allowlist/exclusions; start/end date, windows and holdbacks; platform/channel/device scope and domain allowlist; monetization model; languages/subtitles/dubbing; DRM/geo-block/attribution; fees, revenue share, reporting and tax; warranties, indemnity, audit, takedown, dispute and termination; contract reference/hash, reviewer and renewal date.

Do not infer worldwide rights from a pan-African identity or Africa-wide marketing claim.

## 3. Provider and player architecture — repository audit

The existing repository's docs/streaming-provider-audit.md describes current movie/TV embed providers and explicitly says the audit does not establish real-time uptime, title availability, stream quality or playback success. It also says the player should only embed media Vivid is authorized to distribute or embed.

The existing docs/authorized-direct-streams.md says no direct stream provider is enabled by default. A direct-stream resolver must be explicitly integrated and exact HTTPS hosts allowlisted; it returns null when no authorized stream exists. The document warns browser-side validation is not a complete network security boundary.

**Critical decision:** do not reuse existing movie/anime source IDs or provider list as African Stories providers. They solve different catalogue identity and playback problems, and the repository audit does not prove rights. African Stories needs an isolated provider namespace and server-side rights gate.

### Dedicated African player — required boundaries

1. **African title resolver:** maps title, local title, aliases, year, country, people and source IDs to one canonical Vivid work ID; handles remakes, alternate cuts, multi-part works and transliteration.
2. **Rights/availability service:** returns viewer-specific permitted actions and provider candidates based on title, account, territory, time window, device and licence evidence. Never expose provider secrets or unrestricted source URLs to the client.
3. **Provider registry:** approved first-party/partner integrations only. Record provider identity, protocol, allowed origin/domain, authorization type, territories, licence references, playback types, event contract, captions, DRM/entitlement, health-check and fallback policy.
4. **Playback session broker:** short-lived authorization, server-side token exchange, signed URL handling, entitlement checks and expiry. No API secrets in browser bundles or query strings.
5. **Player runtime:** distinct African player shell supporting approved iframe, authorized HLS/DASH or licensed file delivery. Do not assume every partner offers direct URLs or the same controls.
6. **Audit/telemetry:** record provider selection, entitlement result, failure category and playback lifecycle without unnecessary personal viewing data.
7. **Fail-closed states:** rights unknown/expired, territory denied, provider unavailable, login required, archive request and official external link are distinct outcomes.

### Provider onboarding evidence checklist

Before enabling an adapter: signed agreement or documented rights basis for the exact embed/hosting arrangement; provider confirmation of API/iframe and production domains; machine-readable territory/window; provider test title/account; origin, message schema, CORS, CSP, redirects, cookies, DRM and token refresh docs; captions/audio/accessibility; mobile/desktop compatibility; health/incident contacts; takedown, expiry and emergency-disable procedure.

An iframe load event is not proof of playback. A public player page is not permission. A stream URL found in page source is not an authorized integration.

## 4. Catalogue and metadata strategy

### Source classes

- **Primary archive/institution:** authoritative custody/catalogue record.
- **Rightsholder/distributor:** authoritative rights, title/version and availability.
- **Festival/programme:** screening, award, synopsis and filmmaker lead; usually not rights owner.
- **Broadcaster/platform:** official viewing route or licensing catalogue with territory and term.
- **Research bibliography:** context, criticism, scholarship and filmography.
- **Community/creator submission:** lead requiring verification, not automatically publishable fact.

### Candidate canonical work fields for Phase 3

- work ID, work type, original/display titles, aliases, transliteration, original language, country of origin, production regions, release date and precision, duration, format, version, episode/part;
- people with credited/alternate names, roles, authority identifiers and evidence;
- historical periods, places, communities, themes, genres, languages and subtitle languages;
- synopsis, editorial context, historical claims, source citations, fact-check state and correction log;
- metadata sources with source type, record ID, URI, retrieval date, licence/attribution and confidence;
- assets with creator, source, licence, permitted uses, expiry and attribution;
- archive holdings with institution, reference code, format, physical/digital status, access restrictions, request route and last verification;
- rights grants and provider availability stored separately from descriptive metadata;
- access state derived from current approved evidence.

### Identity and data-quality risks

A film can have multiple titles across scripts and transliteration systems. Dates may refer to production, censorship, festival premiere, theatrical release, broadcast or restoration; store date type and precision. Country of production, director nationality, language, filming location and subject matter are not interchangeable. Festival screening does not prove a public release or rightsholder identity. Film, score, restored version, subtitles, poster and archival source can have different rights. Do not merge records solely on similar titles.

## 5. Partner and acquisition pipeline

**Tier A — archive/public-interest institutions:** Ghana Film Archive and NFA; Nigerian NFC/NFVSA; FESPACO; NARSSA; INICC Mozambique; Uganda Film Unit project partners; Eritrea RDC; Cimatheque and Cinémathèque Tunisienne. Seek metadata, research access, contextual collaboration, digitisation partnerships and rights referrals.

**Tier B — documentary/commercial distributors:** AfriDocs/Steps/Bertha Foundation; MultiChoice Studios; Laba Cinema and specialist distributors. Seek title-level licensing, official watch routes and authorized embeds.

**Tier C — filmmaker-first/emerging platforms:** TuWatch Africa, Kora Stream Africa, True Continent and other aggregation ventures. Commercial terms, current catalogue, legal entity, payment, chain of title and technical claims remain unverified until diligence is complete.

**Tier D — festivals and markets:** FESPACO/MICA, Durban FilmMart, Carthage Film Festival, AFRIFF, Encounters, Africa in Motion, New York African Film Festival and diaspora festivals. Use for discovery, filmmaker contacts and screening history—not as proof the festival owns rights.

For each partner request: catalogue/API and reuse terms; title-level rights contact; territories, languages, windows and monetization; asset permissions; embed/API/stream docs and test credentials; licence model and reporting; archive viewing/reproduction policy and fees; takedown, corrections and preservation process.

No partner is represented as agreed or contracted until written confirmation exists.

## 6. Competitor and positioning implications

The landscape includes curated documentary platforms (AfriDocs), heritage repositories (FESPACO, national archives, Cimatheque, Cinémathèque Tunisienne, Uganda Film Unit project and university collections), commercial sales (MultiChoice Studios and independent distributors), filmmaker-direct streaming/aggregation (TuWatch Africa and emerging services), and research databases (Africine, historical African Film Database, academic guides and filmographies).

Vivid should not claim to replace archives or be the exclusive home of African cinema. Differentiation can be a cross-source discovery layer connecting a work to filmmakers, region, language, historical context, archive holding, official watch route and rights-confirmed player option.
